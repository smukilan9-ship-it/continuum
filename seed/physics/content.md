# Electrostatic potential and capacitance — original Continuum demo seed

## Passage 1 — Electric potential

Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive test charge in bringing it from infinity to that point without changing its kinetic energy. Potential is a scalar property of the field at a location. Its SI unit is the volt, equal to one joule per coulomb.

## Passage 2 — Potential versus potential energy

Potential belongs to the location; potential energy belongs to a particular charge-field system. At a point with potential V, a charge q has potential energy U = qV. Replacing q with 2q doubles U while V at the point remains unchanged, provided the test charge does not alter the source configuration.

## Passage 3 — Equipotential surfaces

The potential difference between any two points on one equipotential surface is zero. Moving a charge along such a surface therefore requires zero work by the electrostatic field. The electric field is perpendicular to an equipotential surface wherever the field is nonzero.

## Passage 4 — Point-charge numerical

For a point charge Q, the potential at distance r in vacuum is V = kQ/r. Potential adds algebraically for a system of point charges because it is scalar.
